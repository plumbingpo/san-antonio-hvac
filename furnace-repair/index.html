A modern furnace runs a strict startup sequence, and knowing where the sequence breaks is most of the diagnosis. The thermostat calls, the inducer motor spins up to prove draft through a pressure switch, the hot surface igniter glows, the gas valve opens, the burners light, the flame sensor confirms fire, and only then does the blower deliver heat to the house. In San Antonio, TX, the failures cluster at predictable points: an oxide-coated flame sensor, a hot surface igniter cracked from years of thermal cycling, a pressure switch that will not close because the inducer is weak or the condensate trap on a high-efficiency model has clogged, or a control board with a burned relay. Each one stops the sequence cold, and each one mimics the others from the hallway.

Rather than guess, San Antonio HVAC tests the sequence one stage at a time. We read igniter resistance, measure flame signal in microamps, check that the pressure switch responds to real inducer draft, confirm voltage at the gas valve, and test the blower and its capacitor. Because this is a fuel-burning appliance, the safety basics come with every visit: we inspect the heat exchanger, leak-test the gas connections, and verify flue draft. You cannot tell a furnace problem from a furnace hazard by looking at the thermostat. Before we leave, temperature rise is measured against the data plate so the fire and the airflow are balanced as the manufacturer intended.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we work on them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are already stocked on the truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. Signs You Need Furnace Repair in San Antonio

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Loud booms at startup caused by delayed ignition
  • A fan that keeps running while the registers blow cool air
  • A pilot or burner flame that is lazy yellow instead of crisp blue
  • Rust streaks, soot, or water around the cabinet or flue
  • The igniter glows and clicks, yet no flame ever appears
  • Short cycling every few minutes without reaching the setpoint
  • Repeated shutdowns just seconds after the burners light
  • Grinding or high-pitched squeals from the blower

Why Professional Furnace Repair Matters

A furnace combines gas, flame, high voltage, and a flue in one cabinet, and the failure that matters most is the one you cannot see from outside: a compromised heat exchanger passing combustion gases into the air your blower pushes through the house. Delayed ignition that booms at light-off stresses that exchanger further every morning it goes unfixed. Meanwhile the diagnostic traps stack up: a furnace that trips its high limit may have a bad switch, or it may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a dying blower, and replacing the switch in the second case just removes a safety while leaving the fault.

At San Antonio HVAC, no symptom gets treated in isolation. A meter tells us the flame signal, so we never condemn a good sensor while the real culprit, a cracked igniter or a weak gas valve, keeps causing trouble. We confirm draft at both the pressure switch and the flue, measure temperature rise against the data plate to catch airflow problems early, and leak-test every gas connection with instruments, not a guess. When we are done, the furnace has proven itself safe and correct through a full cycle, and if it is nearing the end of its useful life, you will get an honest assessment.

Furnace Repair Prevention Tips

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Listen at light-off; clean ignition is quiet, and booming means the burners need service
  • Schedule service each fall to catch worn igniters and dirty sensors before cold weather arrives
  • Keep storage, lint, and especially chlorine-based products away from the furnace closet
  • If you have a high-efficiency furnace, check that the condensate drain is flowing freely
  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
  • Keep a working carbon monoxide alarm on every level of the home and check it each month
  • Make sure the furnace has clear combustion air; a packed utility room starves the burners
